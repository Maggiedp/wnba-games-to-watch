# Methodology

How [wumbers.com](https://wumbers.com) scores WNBA games, what's empirically validated, what's a
design choice, and where the model has known limits. Written to be picked apart.

Every upcoming game gets an **Overall** score out of 100:

```
Overall = 0.60 · Quality + 0.40 · Importance
```

The most important thing to understand up front: **the two inputs are different kinds of claim.**
Importance is built on a win-probability model (Elo) that is *backtested against real outcomes*.
Quality and the 60/40 blend are *editorial* — there is no ground truth for "how watchable is this
game," so they can't be, and aren't, calibrated. The line between the two is drawn explicitly below.

---

## Validated vs. editorial — the honest split

| Component | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Elo win probability** | **Validated** | Time-honest replay, scored against real results (Brier 0.214 over 1,910 games, 2017–2025) |
| **Monte Carlo playoff odds** | **Validated** (inherits Elo) | Built entirely on Elo per-game probabilities |
| Quality (BPI harmonic mean) | Editorial | No ground truth for "good matchup"; the balance-vs-level tradeoff is an un-tunable constant |
| 60/40 Overall weighting | Editorial | No ground truth for "watchability"; the weights are a judgment, not a fit |
| Excitement index | Derived | A transform of ESPN's win-probability feed, not an independent model |

If you only remember one thing: **predictive claims here are backed by proper scoring rules;
editorial claims are labeled as editorial.** We don't dress one up as the other.

---

## Win probability — Elo (validated)

Game-level win probability uses an Elo rating system, replayed chronologically from 2024 each
daily run (no persisted rating state).

- **Time-honest by construction.** Each game is scored on the ratings as they were *before* that
  game — no look-ahead. So replaying the season doubles as an honest backtest.
- **Backtest (2017–2025, 1,910 games):** Brier **0.214**, pick accuracy **67.1%**.
  - Context for the Brier: **0.25** is a coin flip; the practical floor for single basketball games
    is **~0.20** (they are genuinely high-variance). 0.214 is a good score, not a near-coin-flip —
    a number a general audience tends to misread, which is why the public
    [transparency page](https://wumbers.com/transparency) leads with pick accuracy + a reliability
    diagram instead. Here, for a technical reader, it's the headline.
- **Hyperparameters:** `K=16`, home-court `H=+50` Elo, season-boundary regression `0.5`, margin-of-victory
  multiplier on. K and H are joint-optimized by log-loss grid search; the MOV multiplier uses
  FiveThirtyEight-style autocorrelation damping so blowouts by heavy favorites don't run away with
  the ratings.
- **Calibration baseline:** 2016–present, skipping 2020 (COVID bubble — neutral site, no home
  games, so the +50 HCA would distort ratings).

**Caveat — `season_regression = 0.5` is tuned on only 7 season boundaries.** That's a noisy fit for
a continuous hyperparameter. It minimizes early-season Brier across 2016–2025 boundaries, but treat
it as "0.5 ± a fair bit," not a precise optimum. (It's higher than FiveThirtyEight's NBA 1/3 because
the WNBA's ~40-game season leaves end-of-season ratings noisier.)

**Caveat — the backtest figure is a static constant.** The 0.214 / 67.1% on the transparency page is
pasted from `scripts/validate_elo.py`; it is regenerated by hand when Elo hyperparameters or the
historical data change, not computed live.

**Deliberately *not* in the model — rest & travel (we checked).** A common ask is whether back-to-backs,
days of rest, and travel distance should shift the win probability. We investigated it directly
(`scripts/validate_rest_travel.py`): a logistic fit on 2017–2025 (~1,910 games), controlling for the Elo
rating differential, for rest-day differential, back-to-back, travel miles, and timezone crossings. **No
term reached significance** (all p > 0.2), and folding the estimated effects back into the Elo replay did
**not** improve held-out Brier beyond a paired-bootstrap confidence interval (improvement CI straddled
zero). The point estimates have the physically sensible signs (rest helps, B2B and travel hurt) but are
too small to matter once two teams' schedules are differenced — so per our pre-registered ship gate, the
model carries **no rest/travel term**. The tooling stays in the tree to re-check as more seasons accrue.

## Importance — Monte Carlo playoff swing (validated, with a measured bias)

How much does tonight's result move the playoff picture?

- A **single 10,000-run** Monte Carlo simulation plays out the rest of the regular season (and the
  bracket, once it exists), using the Elo win probabilities above for every game.
- We record a full outcome matrix, then **partition** the 10k sims by who won each game and read off
  the conditional playoff rates. Importance for one game is:

  ```
  swing = Σ_teams | P(team makes playoffs | team_a won) − P(team makes playoffs | team_b won) |
  ```

  summed across **all** teams — so a game that decides a bubble team's fate scores high even if
  neither bubble team is playing in it.
- The partition trick is valid because Elo ratings are *frozen* during a sim, so a game's outcome
  doesn't change downstream win probabilities — conditioning on the observed outcome is
  distributionally identical to forcing it. This is why one 10k run suffices instead of
  re-simulating per game.
- Playoff odds shown on the site and the importance score come from the *same* run, so they're
  mutually consistent by construction.

### Corrected bias: Σ|Δ| noise floor (analytic subtraction)

Summing the **absolute value** of finitely-estimated per-team rate differences is positively biased:
even a game that changes nothing has `E[Σ|Δ|] > 0`, because each team's `|rate_a − rate_b|` is the
absolute value of a noisy estimate. We measured it with a placebo bootstrap (re-split the same sims
into same-size buckets *independent* of the game outcome): **≈ 8 normalized importance points
(median 8.2, range 8.0–9.0)**, near-constant across realistic games — 10k sims keep both outcome
buckets in the thousands even for a ~78% favorite, so the floor barely varied and mostly inflated
absolute numbers, not the *ranking*. The games it genuinely distorted were true dead-rubbers (both
teams locked in or eliminated), which scored ~8 instead of ~0.

**Correction (since 2026-06):** under H₀ (outcome independent of a team's fate) each per-team
difference is ≈ Normal(0, p(1−p)(1/n_a + 1/n_b)), so its absolute value is half-normal with mean
`√(2/π)·σ`. We subtract the summed per-team floor

```
Σ_t √(2/π)·√(p_t(1−p_t)·(1/n_a + 1/n_b))      (p_t = pooled rate across both buckets)
```

from each game's raw swing and clamp at 0 (both regular-season playoff-odds swing and postseason
championship swing). Dead-rubbers now score ~0; games with real stakes move by ~the constant the
placebo measured. The same correction runs inside the season-ceiling calibration, so normalized
scores stay on a consistent scale. The per-game placebo bootstrap remains the validation tool, not
the production correction — it's expensive daily and the analytic floor matches it within noise.

The per-team "What's at stake" movers still display **raw** conditional probabilities (correcting
a displayed probability pair would misrepresent the simulation output), so a game whose corrected
swing clamps to 0 suppresses its movers panel entirely — a zero-stakes headline never ships a
stakes panel built from the same noise.

### "No importance score" means *not simulated*, not *zero stakes*

Games outside the simulation universe (e.g. a row not yet classified as regular/postseason) show an
em-dash, not a 0, in the importance column. For the Overall blend, a missing importance is **imputed
with the day's mean importance** — not collapsed to 0, which would falsely assert "zero stakes" and
bury the game in the ranking.

## Quality — BPI harmonic mean (editorial)

Quality answers "is this a good matchup," using the harmonic mean of both teams'
[ESPN BPI](https://www.espn.com/wnba/bpi) ratings (shifted positive first). The harmonic mean
penalizes imbalance: a +5 vs +5 game outscores a +5 vs −5 blowout-in-waiting.

This is a **design choice, not a measurement** — there's no outcome to validate "good matchup"
against. One honest internal knob: the positive-shift offset (currently `+9`, for a fixed BPI range
of ±8) sets *how much* an unbalanced matchup is penalized relative to a low-level one (≈25% of range
for a +6/−6 mismatch, ≈6% for +3/−3). It's a real effect, but its magnitude is a chosen constant,
not a tuned one.

BPI is used **only** for quality. Win probability is Elo, not BPI — the two never mix (mixing them
would require recalibrating both).

## Excitement — a transform of ESPN's win probability (derived)

The post-game **Thriller / Close game** label on completed games is an excitement index:

```
excitement = Σ_i |ΔWP_i| · L_i  (+ a small future term for live games)
```

where `WP` is the win-probability series and `L_i` weights late swings more. It rewards games that
swung a lot, late. **It is computed from ESPN's win-probability feed**, so it inherits whatever
biases ESPN's WP model carries — it measures *movement in ESPN's WP*, not an independent notion of
excitement.

## Known limitations

- **The season sim is underdispersed.** Elo is frozen at today's ratings for the entire simulated
  season, so sims can't produce injury or hot/cold runs. Real seasons have more spread than the
  simulation, so playoff odds are pulled slightly toward the mean (a touch over-confident on good
  teams, under-confident on Cinderellas).
- **The Σ|Δ| importance noise floor** — measured at ~8/100, now corrected with an analytic
  half-normal floor (above); the correction assumes H₀ per team, so strong-signal teams are very
  slightly overcorrected (negligible at 10k sims).
- **`season_regression` is fit on 7 boundaries** and the backtest figure is a hand-regenerated
  constant (both above).
- **No local-blackout awareness.** League Pass blackout eligibility depends on a viewer's DMA/RSN
  maps, which aren't in ESPN's API. The site can show national broadcasters but can't tell an
  individual what they can actually watch.
